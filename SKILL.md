# Research Paper Writing

Make the paper's idea easy to understand and worth reading. Help readers see the situation, the unresolved problem, the proposed response, and what the work contributes. Let the argument develop through sufficient context, clear rationale, concrete explanations, and meaningful takeaways.

Adapt this guidance to the discipline, paper type, author, and venue. It does not prescribe a section order, contribution count, sentence length, or visual template. Use clear-writing when available for prose clarity and continuity; this skill remains usable on its own.

## Communicate the idea and its significance

Identify the central question or problem and explain why it matters. Give readers the context needed to understand what prior work enables, what remains unresolved, and how this paper responds. Make the contribution concrete: what readers learn, what becomes possible, or what understanding changes.

Build a connected argument rather than a list of features or studies. Each part should develop the idea and prepare readers for what follows. A familiar example, figure, or short walkthrough can make the mechanism easier to picture.

Frame the gap fairly without unnecessarily shrinking the work's audience, purpose, or applications. Distinguish the paper's demonstrated task from the broader idea. Present supported contributions confidently; include qualifications where they affect understanding.

## Explain the major decisions

For consequential design, technical, methodological, or analytical choices, connect the need or constraint to the chosen approach and explain why it fits. Discuss alternatives or trade-offs when they clarify the contribution. Put this reasoning near the decision where useful; routine choices need not all receive separate justifications.

Use the author's actual reasoning and materials. If an important rationale is missing, identify the question for the author or clearly offer a possible explanation for discussion.

## Prioritize the reader's main entry points

Give the Abstract, Introduction, Discussion, and Conclusion particular attention. Revisit them as the paper develops so the central idea and takeaway remain clear.

| Section | Main writing priority |
| --- | --- |
| Abstract | Tell a concise, sufficient story: the situation and problem, the approach and contribution, the main findings where available, and why they matter. Make it understandable on its own. |
| Introduction | Establish the context and stakes, explain the gap, and make the proposed response follow naturally. State what the paper contributes and give readers a reason to continue. |
| Discussion | Explain what the findings mean for the original question and existing knowledge. Develop implications and useful insights, rather than simply repeating the results. |
| Conclusion | Leave readers with the contribution and a meaningful takeaway about what the work establishes or enables. |

Also attend to the title, figures, and captions as independent entry points. Methods and results should supply the explanation and evidence needed to understand and assess the work.

## Use familiar terminology and concrete substance

Prefer established terminology from the relevant research and industry. Explain unfamiliar terms where needed and use names consistently. Do not invent labels or rebrand familiar ideas to imply novelty; preserve the author's system names and defined concepts.

Replace generic praise with the actual mechanism, comparison, observation, or insight. Include enough detail to explain what was done and why it matters. Concision should remove repetition and filler while retaining the connections readers need.

## Guide readers through the typeset paper

Treat LaTeX structure as part of the explanation. Prefer concise, informative subheadings; in Results and Discussion, a supported finding or takeaway often makes a useful heading. A standalone central question can make the paper's focus noticeable, and separated participant quotations can make people's experiences easier to follow.

Use these options selectively within the venue's style. Keep figures, captions, comparisons, and their explanations connected. For practical code patterns, layout choices, and accessible structure, consult [references/latex-readability.md](references/latex-readability.md) when working on LaTeX presentation.

## Accuracy and final review

Keep the author's meaning, evidence, terminology, and scope consistent across sections. Verify new literature claims and retain conditions needed to interpret findings. Flag missing evidence or rationale rather than inventing it. For substantive edits, check that the opening promises and closing takeaways match the body, then briefly report consequential changes or unresolved issues.

## Briefly report the guidance used

End with a concise summary of the guidance actually used and its effect, such as clarifying the central idea, explaining a major choice, or adding takeaway headings. Combine this with the normal outcome summary and notes from other skills; respect exact output formats and distinguish proposed edits from applied changes.
